# Working with real Scribe users

Scribe is ready for small, consent-based learning sessions. Treat each session as a chance to find where retrieval, citations, indexing, or the interface fails for a real task. Do not describe planned interviews or internal testing as user adoption. Add a user count to the README only after real people outside the build team have completed a session.

## Recruitment checklist

- Invite people who regularly look up information in a small set of documents. Good starting points include a college department that uses regulations or a small business that relies on operating procedures.
- Explain what Scribe does, that questions and answer ratings are logged, and that uploaded documents remain private to their workspace.
- Ask the participant to choose material they are allowed to share. Avoid personal, confidential, or regulated information unless the organization has approved its use.
- Agree on a short task before the session, such as finding a deadline, comparing two rules, or locating the passage that supports an answer.
- Use the participant's own questions. Do not coach them toward a correct answer or a positive rating.
- Ask before recording audio, screen activity, or identifiable notes. Keep only what is needed to understand the product failure.
- At the end, confirm what may be retained and remove any local notes or sample documents the participant asked you to discard.

## Record each session

Use one entry per session. Keep the notes factual and separate participant observations from your interpretation.

```text
Session date:
Organization and participant role (use a non-identifying label when possible):
Consent and retention limits:
Document types and approximate set size:
Task the participant tried:
Questions asked:
Expected answer and supporting passage:
Observed answer and citations:
Feedback rating and selected reasons:
Where the task succeeded or failed (upload, indexing, retrieval, answer, citation, interface):
Participant's own description of the problem:
Follow-up requested:
Researcher interpretation, kept separate from the observation:
```

Avoid copying document contents or personal details into the session record. Reference the workspace or a redacted example only when necessary to reproduce a problem.

## Monthly failure review

At the end of each month, review submitted ratings with the session notes and trace timeline. Group similar failures, check a sample of the cited passages, and choose a concrete owner and next action. Record counts only when they come from actual feedback records for the stated period.

```text
Month:
Sessions completed:
Feedback records reviewed:

Failure pattern:
Evidence from sessions or trace IDs:
User impact:
Likely stage (upload / indexing / retrieval / answer / citation / interface):
Next action and owner:
Status at the next review:

Patterns that improved:
Unresolved questions:
Changes to test with users:
```

Do not claim users, accuracy, or improvement based on empty templates, seeded data, or internal development activity. Publish adoption only after real user sessions have happened and the count and timeframe can be supported by those records.
